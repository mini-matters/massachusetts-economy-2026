# Massachusetts Economy 2026

## Economic output and employment

Massachusetts has among the highest real GDP per capita of the U.S. states, rising from $79,000 in 2018 to $88,000 in 2024—but that 1.7% growth rate is below the U.S. rate of 1.9%.

Over the same period, nonfarm employment grew about 0.3%, compared with about 1.0% nationally.

Massachusetts' Regional Price Parities ranked 7th—5.8% above the national average—behind California, Hawaii, the District of Columbia, New Jersey, New York, and Washington.

CNBC's "America's Top States for Business 2025" named Massachusetts the second-most-expensive state in which to do business, behind Hawaii, with the highest wage costs in the nation—but also the most-improved state at 20th overall, up eighteen places from 38th in 2024.

The Federal Reserve Bank of Boston attributes New England's constrained employment growth to the size of the labor force and a housing shortage.

## Migration

Massachusetts recorded net domestic out-migration of about 41,000 residents in 2023, while net international migration produced an overall population gain of about 27,000.

The out-migration rate of higher-income households fell from 3.1% in 2021–2022 to 2.7% in 2022–2023.

The Massachusetts Budget and Policy Center attributes the state's outmigration primarily to housing affordability, while some other analyses interpret IRS adjusted-gross-income outflow data as evidence of high-earner departures.

## Cost of housing and energy

The median single-family home price in Greater Boston reached $1,003,250 in June 2025, the first time the regional median exceeded $1 million.

The household income needed to afford an entry-level home in Greater Boston rose from just under $98,000 in 2021 to over $162,000 in 2025.

Massachusetts's average electricity price in 2024 was 23.94 cents per kWh, against a U.S. average of 12.94 cents—about 1.85 times the national price—with an industrial rate of 18.19 cents.

The state's Statewide Housing Needs Assessment identifies a need of roughly 222,000 homes over 2025–2035.

As of January 2026, about 6,900 housing units were in the MBTA Communities Act development pipeline across 34 communities, and the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court upheld the MBTA Communities Act in January 2025.

## Business formation and survival

The Mass Taxpayers Foundation's 2025 Competitiveness Index ranks Massachusetts 49th in new business formations per private-sector employee in 2023, and last—50th—in private-sector employment growth in 2024, which declined 0.6%.

Census Business Formation Statistics show Massachusetts ranked about 47th per capita in new business applications, with about 76,000 applications in 2023.

Concurrently, the state has the highest three-year small-business survival rate in the nation, about 65% for the 2019 cohort.

Forming a limited liability company costs $500, plus a $500 annual report fee, and the Institute for Justice rates Massachusetts among the most burdensome states for licensing lower-income occupations.

## The 2023 income surtax

In November 2022, Massachusetts voters approved the "Fair Share Amendment," a 4% surtax on annual income above $1 million in addition to the state's 5% flat income tax, effective for tax year 2023.

The surtax was budgeted at an initial benchmark of about $1.3 billion per year.

Collections were approximately $2.2–2.5 billion in fiscal year 2024, $3 billion in fiscal year 2025, and exceeded $3.1 billion through the first ten months of fiscal year 2026—about 20% above the prior-year period.

Total Massachusetts tax collections in fiscal year 2025 were $43.7 billion, up 7.1% over fiscal year 2024; the surtax over-collected its benchmark by roughly $1.69 billion, an excess concentrated in capital gains.

Peer-reviewed research on state millionaire taxes finds small migration responses to such taxes.

## State tax on a founder's exit

The state tax owed on a long-term capital gain from selling startup stock depends on the founder's state of residence at the time of sale; federal tax (a 20% rate plus the 3.8% net investment income tax) is the same in every state.

Massachusetts taxes long-term capital gains at the 5% flat income rate, plus the 4% surtax on taxable income above $1,083,150 in 2025 ($1,107,750 in 2026), for a top effective rate of about 9%.

California taxes capital gains as ordinary income at a top rate of 13.3%—a 12.3% top bracket plus a 1% surtax on income over $1 million. New York taxes capital gains as ordinary income at a top state rate of 10.9% (on income over $25 million), and a New York City resident pays up to an additional 3.876%, for a combined top rate of about 14.8%. Texas has no individual income tax and imposes no state tax on capital gains.

On a hypothetical $100 million long-term gain, the approximate state tax is therefore as follows:
Massachusetts at ~9.0%, California at 13.3%, New York (NYC resident) at ~14.8%; Texas at 0%.

## Venture capital and artificial intelligence

Massachusetts companies raised $16.7 billion in venture capital in 2025 (up about 12% from 2024), while venture funding rose 82% in California, 72% in Texas, 48% in Florida, and 22% in New York.

Global AI venture capital in 2025 was approximately $211 billion; the San Francisco Bay Area received about 60% (roughly $126 billion), and Greater Boston an estimated 3–4%.

OpenAI, Anthropic, and xAI are headquartered in the San Francisco Bay Area.

Liquid AI, a Boston foundation-model company spun out of MIT's CSAIL, raised a $250 million Series A at a $2.35 billion valuation in December 2025.

In the first quarter of 2026, startups in Texas raised more venture capital ($5.8 billion) than startups in Massachusetts ($5.3 billion), described as the first time on record.

## Life sciences

Kendall Square in Cambridge has been described as the densest square mile of technology and biotechnology research.

Massachusetts ranked second among U.S. states in biopharma venture capital in 2025, behind California.

Its biopharma companies raised $6.85 billion in venture capital that year—the lowest since 2019 and a fourth consecutive annual decline—equal to 26.2% of U.S. biopharma venture dollars, against California's 43.3%.

The number of drugs in development by Massachusetts firms grew approximately 14% year over year in 2025.

Greater Boston laboratory vacancy rose through 2025 into 2026, reported at approximately 29.7% in the second quarter of 2025 and approximately 34.1 percent by early 2026, with newly built lab space delivered without tenants.

## Quantum computing

MIT operates a Center for Quantum Engineering that engages more than eighty PIs, and Harvard runs the Harvard Quantum Initiative.

A Harvard-led team reported the first programmable logical quantum processor, with up to forty-eight logical qubits, using a neutral-atom array. A Harvard/MIT team later demonstrated continuous operation of a 3,000-plus-qubit neutral-atom system for over two hours. An MIT group reported a record single-qubit gate fidelity of 99.998% on a fluxonium qubit.

NVIDIA announced in March 2025 that it would site its Accelerated Quantum Computing Research Center in Boston.

QuEra, a Boston company spun out of Harvard and MIT, raised a $230 million round in 2025 and was selected for Stage B of DARPA's Quantum Benchmarking Initiative in November 2025.

Atlantic Quantum, an MIT spinout in Cambridge developing fluxonium superconducting qubits, was selected for Stage A of DARPA's Quantum Benchmarking Initiative in April 2025, and its team joined Google Quantum AI in October 2025, ending its independent operation.

Other Massachusetts quantum companies include CavilinQ (Cambridge; an $8.8 million seed round in 2026), Qunett, Quantum Diamond Technologies, MagiQ Technologies, and Quantum Microwave Components; Lightsynq was acquired by IonQ in June 2025, and Zapata Computing ceased operations in October 2024.

The Healey administration brands its quantum economic-development effort "Quantum Massachusetts" through MassTech.

In October 2024 it awarded $4,994,520 to establish a state-funded quantum computing complex at the Massachusetts Green High Performance Computing Center in Holyoke, deploying a QuEra computer.

In October 2025 it awarded UMass Boston $3.8 million for a statewide quantum collaborative.

In May 2026 it committed up to $25 million to a new MIT Quantum Systems Laboratory.

No Massachusetts region holds a federal Tech Hub designation for quantum; the federal quantum Tech Hub, Elevate Quantum, is in Colorado.

## Federal research and defense institutions

MIT Lincoln Laboratory, in Lexington, is the largest U.S. defense research-and-prototyping FFRDC, with approximately 4,500 staff.

MIT's Air Force contract to operate it was valued at up to $12.2 billion, announced in April 2025 and running through 2030.

Draper, a Cambridge engineering organization with annual revenue of approximately $0.8–0.9 billion, holds a potential five-year, $2.2 billion U.S. Navy contract for the Trident II guidance subsystem.

Boston received about $2.456 billion in NIH funding in fiscal year 2024—the second-highest of any U.S. city, 71% of Massachusetts's approximately $3.46 billion (3rd among states), and the highest per capita of any city.

## Fusion energy

Commonwealth Fusion Systems, headquartered in Devens, Massachusetts, is an MIT spinout.

It raised an $863 million Series B2 round in August 2025, bringing total funding to close to $3 billion—described as about one-third of all private fusion capital raised worldwide.

Google signed a power-purchase agreement with Commonwealth Fusion Systems.

The company's first commercial power plant, ARC, is sited in Chesterfield County, Virginia.

## Robotics

iRobot, based in Bedford, Massachusetts, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in December 2025 and is being taken private by its contract manufacturer, Picea Robotics of China.

## State and federal policy actions, 2025–2026

The Mass Leads Act, signed in 2024, authorized approximately $3.5–4 billion for life sciences, artificial intelligence, climatetech, and advanced manufacturing.

A February 2025 federal policy capping NIH indirect research-cost reimbursement at 15% was struck down, and the First Circuit affirmed the block on January 5, 2026.

A federal court in Massachusetts struck down a $100,000 fee on new H-1B visas on June 8, 2026; the administration stated it would appeal.

Medicaid changes in the 2025 federal reconciliation law are projected to reduce federal health-care funding to Massachusetts by about $3.5 billion annually once fully in effect.

A 100% tariff on patented pharmaceuticals and their ingredients is scheduled to take effect July 31, 2026, under an April 2026 Section 232 proclamation.

On December 22, 2025, the federal government paused construction at five in-progress offshore-wind projects, including Vineyard Wind, citing a national-security review.

## Regional differences

"Gateway Cities" is the term used by MassINC for Massachusetts's mid-size former industrial cities, including Springfield, Worcester, New Bedford, Fall River, Lawrence, Brockton, and Lowell.

From 2018 to 2023, real GDP in Greater Boston (Suffolk and Middlesex counties) grew roughly four times faster than in the Hampden, Bristol, and Berkshire county group, the last of which contracted over that period.
